ROUGH MATERIALS AND METHODS
HIGH-RESOLUTION GAS DISPERSION MEASUREMENTS IN A DAIRY BARN
======================================================================

Study objective
---------------

The study investigated the spatial heterogeneity of gas concentrations
inside a dairy barn. The principal gases considered were carbon dioxide
(CO2), methane (CH4), and ammonia (NH3). Water vapour (H2O) was retained
as an additional measured variable.

The sampling design was progressively simplified over four measurement
campaigns. The first campaign used a detailed three-dimensional sampling
layout. Results and practical experience from each campaign were then
used to select the locations retained in the following campaign.


Study site and barn configuration
---------------------------------

The measurements were conducted in a dairy barn housing a herd of 58
cows. The barn had approximate external dimensions of 38.8 m x 17.6 m.
The building included lying cubicles, walking and manure alleys, a manure
scraper, a manure pit, a milking area, and an adjacent young-calf housing
area.

The number and spatial distribution of cows inside the barn varied during
the measurements because some cows moved between the barn and an outdoor
exercise yard. The number of cows present and relevant management
activities were documented during the campaigns.

Air movement and gas dispersion could be influenced by the barn geometry,
building openings, mechanically operated circulation fans, water
sprinklers, manure handling, and the distribution and activity of the
animals. Fan operation and sprinkler operation were documented. Outdoor
wind conditions were recorded using a meteorological mast at a height of
10 m above ground level.


Spatial gas-sampling system
---------------------------

A multi-point tube-sampling system was installed to characterize the
spatial heterogeneity of gas concentrations inside the barn. In the
initial configuration, 17 horizontal sampling positions were distributed
across the principal animal-occupied and manure-handling areas.

At each horizontal position, air was sampled at three heights:

1. Top: approximately 60 cm below the roof.
2. Middle: approximately 360 cm above the floor.
3. Bottom: approximately 260 cm above the floor.

The first campaign therefore contained 51 barn sampling locations. The
locations were numbered in vertical groups of three:

- Top locations: 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28, 31, 34, 37,
  40, 43, 46, and 49.
- Middle locations: 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 32, 35,
  38, 41, 44, 47, and 50.
- Bottom locations: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36,
  39, 42, 45, 48, and 51.

The sampling tubes were routed through common distribution or ring lines.
Additional reference measurements were retained when available. The
internal/reference sampling position was standardized as "s", and the
outside sampling position was standardized as "out".


Gas analysers
-------------

Concentrations were measured using Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR)
spectroscopy and cavity ring-down spectroscopy (CRDS). Depending on the
campaign, the measurement system consisted of two FTIR analysers and two
CRDS analysers, or only the two CRDS analysers.

The final cleaned data contained the following variables:

DATE.TIME, analyser, location, CO2, CH4, NH3, and H2O.

CO2, CH4, and NH3 were retained in ppm. H2O was retained in volume
percent.


Sampling and averaging procedure
--------------------------------

The CRDS analysers measured each multiplexer position over a nominal
240-second interval. The first 60 seconds following a change in sampling
position were treated as flushing time and excluded. Measurements from
the remaining part of the interval were averaged to obtain one
concentration value for the corresponding location.

Previously cleaned CRDS files were not flushed or averaged a second time
when the campaign datasets were combined.

FTIR measurements were imported from the original instrument exports.
Repeated embedded header lines and other non-measurement records were
removed. The instrument date and time fields were combined into a single
timestamp, and only the required gas variables were retained. No
additional flushing period was applied to the FTIR records.

All timestamps were processed in the Europe/Berlin time zone. Overlapping
CRDS exports were deduplicated using the combination of DATE.TIME,
analyser, and location.


Campaign 1: complete three-height layout
----------------------------------------

Processed measurement period:
1 October 2024 at 00:02:32 to 24 October 2024 at 14:38:58.

The first campaign used the complete set of 51 barn locations. Seventeen
horizontal positions were sampled at the top, middle, and bottom heights.
Two FTIR analysers and two CRDS analysers were used.

The analyser-specific location allocation was:

- CRDS9: locations 1-16.
- FTIR2: locations 17-26.
- CRDS8: locations 27-42.
- FTIR1: locations 43-51.
- FTIR1 source position 10: internal/reference location "s".

The purpose of this campaign was to characterize the initial
three-dimensional concentration distribution and determine whether all
three heights contributed independent spatial information.


Campaign 2: removal of the middle height
----------------------------------------

Processed measurement period:
16 November 2024 at 00:06:47 to 31 December 2024 at 23:57:10.

Evaluation of the first campaign indicated that the middle-height
locations provided comparatively limited additional information for the
subsequent spatial mapping. All 17 middle-height locations were therefore
removed.

Locations 19 and 40 were also excluded. Location 19 was close to a
circulation fan and was considered likely to be strongly influenced by
the local fan flow. Location 40 was affected by leakage in its sampling
line.

The remaining 32 top and bottom locations were assigned serially to the
two CRDS analysers:

- CRDS9 MPVPositions 1-16:
  locations 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18, 21, 22, 24,
  and 25.

- CRDS8 MPVPositions 1-16:
  locations 27, 28, 30, 31, 33, 34, 36, 37, 39, 42, 43, 45, 46,
  48, 49, and 51.


Campaign 3: reduced CRDS sampling configuration
------------------------------------------------

Processed measurement period:
19 August 2025 at 13:00:12 to 19 November 2025 at 09:06:42.

The third campaign used a reduced and modified set of top and bottom
locations based on the findings and practical experience from the
previous campaigns. Measurements were conducted using CRDS8 and CRDS9.

The saved numeric locations in the segregated third-campaign files were
preserved. The source reference label "in" was standardized as "s", and
the source label "S" was standardized as "out". These reference
measurements were retained rather than discarded.

Most third-campaign source files contained 240-second averages. A supplied
450-second-average file was used as provided and was not averaged again.


Campaign 4: seven selected bottom locations
-------------------------------------------

Processed measurement period:
9 December 2025 at 12:03:59 to 21 July 2026 at 02:38:20.

The fourth campaign focused on seven bottom-height locations. These
locations were selected pragmatically according to their positions
relative to the ring-line orifices and included a position near the
centre of the barn.

The CRDS8 MPVPosition-to-location mapping was:

- MPVPosition 1 -> location 12.
- MPVPosition 2 -> location 18.
- MPVPosition 3 -> location 15.
- MPVPosition 4 -> location 30.
- MPVPosition 5 -> location 36.
- MPVPosition 6 -> location 42.
- MPVPosition 7 -> location 45.
- MPVPosition 8 ("in") -> reference location "s".
- MPVPosition 9 ("S") -> reference location "out".

The fourth-campaign folder contained overlapping CRDS exports. Repeated
records were removed before the campaign dataset was finalized.


Supporting operational and environmental data
----------------------------------------------

The interpretation of spatial gas concentrations considered operational,
animal, and environmental conditions that could influence short-term gas
dispersion. The supporting records included:

- the number and distribution of cows inside the barn;
- movement of cows between the barn and exercise yard;
- circulation-fan operation;
- water-sprinkler operation;
- manure scraper and other manure-handling activity; and
- wind speed and wind direction measured at 10 m above ground level.

These variables can be used to distinguish persistent spatial
concentration patterns from short-term variability associated with
ventilation, animal activity, management operations, and outdoor weather
conditions.
