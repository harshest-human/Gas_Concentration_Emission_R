# Manuscript 3 concept

## Central question

How are CO2, CH4 and NH3 concentrations and their ratios distributed across horizontal functional zones and vertical heights in a naturally ventilated dairy barn, and what practical sampling principles follow from that heterogeneity?

## Hypothesis

Gas concentrations and gas ratios exhibit systematic spatial structure with sampling height and horizontal position inside the barn.

## Engineering consequence

Alternative spatial coverage, vertical coverage, density and temporal aggregation can change representative indoor concentrations and thereby propagate into indirect CO2-balance ventilation and emission estimates. This is evaluated as a consequence of heterogeneity, not as a hypothesis.

## Boundaries

- The paper does not claim a universal optimal network for all barns.
- Concentration representativeness is not automatically flux representativeness.
- Gas ratios diagnose shared or divergent behaviour but do not prove complete mixing.
- The standard-input CO2-balance exercise isolates sensitivity to concentration sampling; it is not direct validation of absolute barn emissions.
- Campaign 2 is supporting seasonal evidence, not a replication of the dense Campaign 1 design.
